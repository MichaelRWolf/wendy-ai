# Conversation Starters: From Facebook Sales Campaigns Guidance

Based on the ChatGPT conversation in `facebook_sales_campaigns.md`. These starters are grounded in the simple, tactical guidance there--before adding bigger questions about revenue or business model.

---

## Scope: Facebook Ads Testing (Phase 1)

These start from the straightforward principle in the guidance: "separate 'getting it running' from 'building the ideal tracking system' because Meta makes it harder than necessary."

---

## Starter 1: On the Simple Test vs. Polish

**From the guidance:** "For a modest first test: $5-10 per day for 4-7 days."

**Michael could say:**

"I noticed the guidance suggests starting really small--just $5-10/day for a few days to see what happens. No Pixels yet, no fancy tracking. How do you feel about that? Would that feel like enough to learn something, or does it feel too small to bother?"

**Why this works:**

- Honors the "start simple" principle from the guidance
- Doesn't assume she'll do it or won't
- Opens dialogue about pace and risk tolerance

---

## Starter 2: On the Message-First Principle

**From the guidance:** "The bigger issue isn't technology--it's finding the message that reliably makes the right people say 'Yes.' Technology amplifies. It rarely rescues."

**Michael could say:**

"There's this line in the guidance that stuck with me: 'Technology amplifies. It rarely rescues.' Before we worry about Pixels or tracking, the question is whether the *invitation itself* is landing. Do you want to test that first?"

**Why this works:**

- Introduces the core principle without being preachy
- Frames it as something from the guidance, not his opinion
- Asks if she wants to prioritize message before mechanics

---

## Starter 3: On Testing the New Language

**From the guidance:** "You and Wendy have spent time discovering a new way to talk about the work. Don't change ten things at once. Test the new language first. If registrations improve, you've learned something valuable."

**Michael could say:**

"You and I have been working on how to *talk* about what you do--the new language we've been developing. The guidance suggests testing that language first, before changing anything else. Just seeing if that new way of speaking gets a different response. Does that appeal to you?"

**Why this works:**

- References work they've already done together
- Frames testing as natural, not radical
- Emphasizes "test the language" not "fix the whole system"

---

## Starter 4: On the Traffic Objective

**From the guidance:** "Choose Traffic as the objective... Spend $5-10/day for a few days. See what happens."

**Michael could say:**

"The simplest version is: write a good invitation, put it on a landing page, run a small Facebook ad sending people to it ($5/day for a week), and just watch what happens. No optimization yet. Just--does anyone click? Does anyone show up? That's Phase 1. Does that sound doable?"

**Why this works:**

- Breaks down the concrete steps (clear and not overwhelming)
- Uses "Phase 1" language from the guidance (which she's already familiar with from the bootcamp concept)
- "Just watch what happens" is experimental, not judgmental

---

## Starter 5: On Not Starting with Tracking

**From the guidance:** "Don't touch: Pixels, conversions, Events Manager yet."

**Michael could say:**

"Here's what I like about this approach: we're not installing Pixels or building complex tracking right away. We're just doing the ad and watching. If nothing happens, we didn't build infrastructure for nothing. If something *does* happen, *then* we can get smarter about measuring it. Does that feel right?"

**Why this works:**

- Validates the "don't over-build" principle
- Protects against the risk of sunk time in infrastructure
- Respects her time investment

---

## Starter 6: On the Measurement Framework

**From the guidance:** "Track only these numbers initially: Ad reach, Landing-page views, Registrations, Attendees, First-time attendees, People who return."

**Michael could say:**

"There's a simple measurement idea here: just track--how many people saw it, clicked it, registered, actually showed up. Nothing fancy. That tells you where the biggest gap is (is it the ad, the page, the registration form, or the reminder before the event?). Does that kind of simple tracking feel manageable?"

**Why this works:**

- Offers concrete, bounded metrics (not overwhelming)
- Shows what data tells you
- Frames it as diagnostic, not judgment

---

## Starter 7: On the Timeline

**From the guidance:** "For a modest first test: $5-10 per day for 4-7 days."

**Michael could say:**

"The timeline is short--spend maybe $50 total over a week, see what data you get. Not a big commitment. Does a one-week test feel like a reasonable way to learn something?"

**Why this works:**

- Makes the time/money commitment concrete and small
- Frames it as exploratory
- Asks if pace feels right

## Part 2: Conversation Extenders: Bridging to Bigger Questions

These build on the Facebook ads conversation but gently bring in Michael's concerns (don't build tech for $0 revenue) and her requests (from other directories) for help with marketing/offers/revenue.

---

## Extender 1: After Phase 1 Completes (Or Starts)

**If she runs Phase 1 and gets results:**

**Michael could say:**

"Okay, so people clicked and registered. That's good data. Now--what's the next question? Is it 'how do we get more people to see it?' Or is it 'do these registrants actually *want* to buy something vs. just showing up for free?' Because the answer changes what we do next."

**Why this works:**

- Assumes success (not judgmental)
- Points toward the customer-demand question without lecturing
- Respects her to choose the direction

---

## Extender 2: If No Results from Phase 1

**If Phase 1 shows very low engagement:**

**Michael could say:**

"Okay, so this version didn't land. Before we build more, I want to ask: what do you think didn't work? Was it the people we targeted, the message, the offer itself, the price (if there was one), or the timing? Because each one needs a different fix."

**Why this works:**

- Doesn't assume failure is permanent
- Asks for her diagnosis first
- Makes it systematic (pick one thing to change)
- Sets up for fast iteration

---

## Extender 3: Bridging to Offers & Revenue

**Once there's traction from ads/registrations:**

**Michael could say:**

"You mentioned wanting help with 'marketing, offers, and marketing them.' We've now got a way to get people's attention (the Phase 1 test worked). So the next thing I'm curious about--when those people show up, what are you actually selling them? Or is it still 'come to this free event and see what happens'? Because that changes how we think about what comes next."

**Why this works:**

- References her explicit request for help with offers
- Doesn't assume the offer exists yet
- Asks the fundamental question: what's the customer buying?

---

## Extender 4: On Technology Investment vs. Revenue

**Only if she wants to build more infrastructure before knowing if anyone will pay:**

**Michael could say:**

"I notice we might be heading toward building more sophisticated tracking, automations, or systems. Before we do that, I want to ask: do we know yet if anyone will actually *pay* for what you're offering? Or are we still in 'test the message' mode? Because I don't want to invest time building infrastructure until we know there's revenue to support it."

**Why this works:**

- Directly states his concern without accusation
- Frames it as a practical question, not judgment
- Protects both their time
- Points toward revenue question naturally

---

## Extender 5: On the Business Model

**If/when she's ready to discuss scaling beyond Phase 1:**

**Michael could say:**

"We've tested the message, we've seen some engagement. Now I want to understand the business part: Who specifically pays? How much? What are they buying--a one-time workshop, an ongoing program, something else? Because that determines whether this is worth scaling or if we need to change the offer itself."

**Why this works:**

- Doesn't accuse her of not having a business model
- Just asks for clarity
- Frames it as necessary info for next steps
- Opens to revenue conversation naturally

---

## Extender 6: On Time Investment (Gentle)

**If infrastructure building seems to be taking over:**

**Michael could say:**

"I want to check in: how much time do you want to spend on marketing/ads versus actually doing the work you love? Because we can keep optimizing these tests, but I don't want us to spend a year on this if we're not seeing traction. What's a reasonable timeframe for you to decide 'this is working' or 'we need to pivot'?"

**Why this works:**

- Acknowledges both sides (marketing matters, but so does her actual work)
- Asks for her boundary on time/investment
- Opens to hard-stop conversation naturally

---

## Extender 7: Bringing in Her Other Requests

**Referencing her requests from other directories (marketing, offers, revenue):**

**Michael could say:**

"In the other conversations, you've talked about wanting help with 'marketing, offers, and marketing them.' This Facebook ads thing is the first concrete test of that. What I want to make sure is--we're not just doing ads for ads' sake. We're doing ads to find out: is there actually a market for what you're offering, and what are they willing to pay? Is that the right question to answer first?"

**Why this works:**

- Connects the immediate work to her bigger ask
- Names the real goal (market discovery + revenue)
- Respectfully challenges whether that's the right focus
- Doesn't assume she's thought about it this way yet

---

## Extender 8: On Message Refinement vs. Tech Build

**If she wants to start building before message is tested:**

**Michael could say:**

"I keep coming back to that principle: 'Technology amplifies. It rarely rescues.' What if we commit to testing the *message* first--just the ads, the invitation, seeing if people respond--before we build anything else? That way we know we're building the right thing."

**Why this works:**

- Uses her own guidance against tech-first urge
- Proposes a bounded commitment (test message first)
- Protects against building wrong solution

## How to Use These (Conversation Flow)

1. **Start with Starters 1-7** -- grounded in the Facebook guidance, tactical, no big questions yet
2. **Use Extenders only if/when:**
   - Phase 1 actually runs and produces data, OR
   - She asks for help with marketing/offers/revenue, OR
   - She wants to build infrastructure before knowing if anyone will pay
3. **Extender 3-7 point toward** the business questions (offer, customer, revenue) without accusing or lecturing
4. **Listen for openings** -- if she volunteers "I want to know if anyone will actually buy this," then you can go deeper into revenue testing
5. **Stay boundaried** -- your role is to ask good questions, not run her business
