# Conversation Starters: Things Michael Could Actually Say

These are grounded in what Wendy has already said. They ask questions, not conclusions. They respect her "don't tell me what to do" boundary.

---

## Starter 1: On the "Easier and Faster" Goal

**What she said:** "of course i would like to make bigger impact and of course i want it all to be easier and faster"

**Michael could ask:**

"When you say you want it easier and faster--easier/faster at what specifically? The content creation? Getting it into the world? Getting people to actually see/use it? Because I want to make sure we're solving for the right friction."

**Why this works:**

- Starts from her goal, not his judgment
- Opens dialogue instead of stating a problem
- Respects her autonomy to define what "easier" means

---

## Starter 2: On the 60 Hours of Video

**What she said:** "i prolly have about 60 hours of video - but they need a bit of attention to go into the world"

**Michael could ask:**

"You've got 60 hours of video that need 'a bit of attention.' What does 'into the world' mean to you? Like--who would watch this, and what would they do after? Because that might tell us what kind of attention it actually needs."

**Why this works:**

- Acknowledges the real work (60 hours exists)
- Doesn't assume what "going into the world" means
- Gently moves toward: "who's the audience?"

---

## Starter 3: On the Central Data Hub

**What she said:** "i would like to put all my info together... there is a one place data you can look at and we can work from there to do all the things smarter: website, marketing, offers and marketing them"

**Michael could ask:**

"I'm tracking that you want one place to pull from for website, marketing, offers, and marketing them. Once we've got that organized--how will we know if it's actually working? Like, what would 'working' look like? What would success feel like versus just... a really well-organized database?"

**Why this works:**

- Validates the organizing impulse (she wants structure)
- Asks what comes AFTER organization
- Doesn't imply she's been wrong to focus on organization
- Opens the door to: "well, people actually buying it" without saying it first

---

## Starter 4: On the Clone/AI Concept

**What she said:** "ive been want to make something like they are calling a clone... we can work from there to do all the things smarter"

**Michael could ask:**

"The clone idea appeals to you--centralized, organized, you can reference it to do everything smarter. I get it. One question: In your mind, what's the clone *for*? Like, once we've built it, what does it do? Who uses it? What problem does it solve for someone?"

**Why this works:**

- Doesn't critique the clone idea (she likes it)
- Asks for her vision of the end state
- Points toward: customer/market need without being preachy
- Respectfully asks: "what's the business model here?"

---

## Starter 5: On the "Make It Smarter" Request

**What she said:** "we can work from there to do all the things smarter: website, marketing, offers and marketing them"

**Michael could ask:**

"I want to help you make it smarter. Before we dive in--what's one place where you're stuck right now? Like, if you had to pick the one thing that feels hardest or most blocked, what would it be? Because I don't want to build systems for problems you're not actually experiencing."

**Why this works:**

- Offers help without assuming what's needed
- Asks her to prioritize (respects her judgment)
- Signals that not everything needs to be fixed at once
- Moves toward: "what's the actual bottleneck?"

---

## Starter 6: The Revenue Question (Soft)

**When/if there's an opening about marketing or offers:**

**Michael could say:**

"You want to do offers and marketing them smarter. I'm curious--when you think about that, what does success look like to you? Like, more people aware of it? More people trying it? People actually paying? I just want to make sure I understand what you're optimizing for, because the answer changes what we build."

**Why this works:**

- Asks for her definition of success
- Doesn't assume she cares about revenue (she might care about reach, impact, participation)
- Doesn't accuse, just clarifies
- Plants the idea that "people actually paying" is a valid measure without forcing it

---

## Starter 7: The Autonomy Reaffirmation

**If she seems defensive about any of this:**

**Michael could say:**

"I know you don't like being told what to do, and I'm not trying to do that. I just want to understand what *you* want so I can actually be useful instead of just building something and hoping it's right. What would be helpful?"

**Why this works:**

- Directly acknowledges her stated boundary
- Validates it ("don't like being told" not "resistant" or "difficult")
- Positions him as support, not director
- Asks her to set the terms

---

## Starter 8: The Small-Scale Test Opener

**If an opening appears after organizing phase:**

**Michael could ask:**

"Once we've got the data organized and the messaging tighter, what would be a small way to test if it's landing with people? Like, not a big launch--something low-risk that would tell us if people are actually interested?"

**Why this works:**

- Assumes success of organization phase (not judgmental)
- Frames testing as *checking*, not proving something failed
- "Small," "low-risk" respects her pacing
- Gently introduces the idea: "test demand" without calling it a business
- Doesn't jump to "measure revenue"--just "are people interested?"

---

## Starter 9: The Time/Investment Honesty

**Only if/when trust is there and she brings up time/effort herself:**

**Michael could say:**

"I notice we're talking about a lot of things--videos, website, marketing, books, offers. I want to make sure we're not just trading one never-ending refinement cycle for another. What if we picked *one* thing that matters most to you right now and nailed that first? Then see what opens up?"

**Why this works:**

- Doesn't accuse her of endless refinement
- Positions it as "I notice" not "you do"
- Offers a concrete alternative (one thing first)
- Respects her time without saying "you're wasting time"
- Opens to: sustainable pace, visible results

---

## What These Starters Have in Common

✅ Start from what she actually said
✅ Ask questions, not conclusions  
✅ Respect her autonomy ("don't tell me what to do" boundary)
✅ Don't assume she's wrong about anything yet
✅ Gently move the conversation toward: audience, impact, willingness to test
✅ Plant ideas (like measuring revenue) without forcing them
✅ Can be used naturally in conversation without sounding scripted

---

## What They Don't Do

❌ Tell her she's been wrong for 5-15 years
❌ Assume the problem is "hobby mindset"
❌ Jump straight to "you need to measure revenue"
❌ Frame organizing/content-creation as wasted work
❌ Lecture about business models
❌ Make her feel like her current direction is bad
❌ Suggest she needs a "real marketer" (unless she brings it up)

---

## How to Use These

- Pick 1-2 that feel natural for the next conversation
- Use them to open dialogue, not to make a point
- Listen more than you talk after asking
- If she says something that resonates with your concern (like "I want to know if people actually want this"), *then* you can share the framework
- Let her lead; these just keep the conversation honest
