# Michael's Push Back: Business vs. Hobby

## The Core Tension

I am happy to support content creation as a hobby, a life goal, a spiritual path, leading a fulfilled life, understanding oneself or the world or humanity. These are noble pursuits. But noble only when Maslow has a solid base.

I do not see earlier activities (book writing ideas, retreat centers, courses, multi-year curriculum design) as business ideas. They are having a full life. They are not a business.

There's an always-never-good-enough cycle: refine the ideas, polish the material, write newsletters (that don't get read), create posts (that don't prompt action). This feels like content creation and rethinking ideas is a guaranteed means to customer acquisition. It is not.

## The Honest Assessment

**I cannot tell someone:** "Invest $x in Wendy's business and it will return $x × some-number-greater-than-one."

Any money into this would be more like Patreon--someone supporting a lifestyle, not trading value for value.

**What I observe (and I admit I don't observe everything, and can't know her inner heart):** It is not a business I would invest in.

## The Business Problem, Stated Simply

**Proof:** There is too much focus on process and product, and nearly none on acquiring customers.

- Finding out where customers are
- Creating offerings with real $$$$ amounts (not just "register and maybe attend" free events with nearly zero new faces)
- Testing whether people will actually pay

It's a great social club--a group of familiar faces. It is not a business.

## My Own Complicity

I know that I am generating exactly $0 of new business myself, so I don't want to point a finger at a skill I also lack.

But putting both of our focus on a task that is guaranteed by design to not have revenue goals is a way to guarantee that zero revenue will follow.

**I do not believe that "if you do what you love, the money will follow."** The marketing phrase is closer to truth: "If nothing happens, nothing will."

## The Gap I Cannot Close

I think she needs a better marketer to get her more visibility and exposure (and therefore revenue). That marketer is not me.

I can get her better tech that yields the same response. But better tech solving the wrong problem is still the wrong answer.

## What I'm Willing To Do

I am happy to invest some time in her "business" because I want to support her full life. But I am not willing to invest the kind of time in technical polishing (creating campaigns, automating workflows that produce $0 revenue, creating SOPs that get followed but generate no business).

I love her to the moon as a partner and life mate. But I do not (yet) see that working together on this project will yield sustainable income.

## The Hope

Maybe the recent switches are different. But I need a gentle on-ramp so that I don't feel burned by investing in something that I don't believe in. And I don't believe that most of her activities are likely to make money.

---

*Wrap my marriage and own insecurities around the "Build a better mousetrap and nobody will buy it" way of thinking about the value of technology, but with a sense that someone else knows how to market her wonderfulness.*
